Researched 2026-10-19 from public pages only. Every fact links to its source; raw rows are in [competitors.csv](competitors.csv).

**Limits of this research, up front:**
- Capterra, G2, Software Advice and Google Play blocked direct reading, and Reddit was not reachable. Review quotes below come from **search-engine summaries of those review pages**, not from reading each review, so the counts are approximate.
- No competitor publishes a Chennai-specific price. No Chennai-only FSM vendor turned up; a Google Maps or JustDial search in person would be the next check.
- **Nothing here shows what Chennai shops actually use today.** That needs `/founder-consumer` and, better, conversations with real owners.

## 2. Price range for a 10-technician shop, per month

Prices are per user, per company or per appointment depending on the vendor, so this is a rough comparison, not like-for-like.

| | Product | Monthly cost for ~10 technicians |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest | WhatsApp, paper, TeamShastra Starter, Zoho FSM Free | **₹0** (Zoho's free plan stops at 30 appointments a month) |
| Low paid | Quick Assign | ₹699 flat |
| Low paid | TeamShastra Growth | ≈ ₹990 + GST |
| Middle | Zoho FSM Standard | US$30 covers only 60 appointments. A shop doing several jobs per technician a day would buy more appointments, priced by volume (not published) |
| Middle (median of the paid options) | Fieldproxy (reported) | ≈ ₹4,000–8,000 |
| High | Zoho via reseller at ₹1,000/user | ≈ ₹10,000 + GST |
| Highest | FieldEZ | ≈ US$250 |

**What this means for pricing:** the floor is free, and India-built tools already sell for **₹70–100 per technician per month**. A new product has to justify a price above TeamShastra and Quick Assign with an outcome the owner can see, not with a longer feature list. `/founder-pricing` takes it from here.

## 4. What their customers complain about

Only Zoho FSM had enough public reviews to read (Capterra shows 4.6 from 43 reviews). The quotes below come from search-engine summaries of [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10014004/Zoho-FSM/reviews/) and [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/zoho-fsm/reviews) review pages. Most reviewers were positive overall.

| # | Theme | Mentions seen | Quotes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The technician app is cluttered or needs a constant connection** | 4 | "not very UX/UI friendly, far too much options"; needs "a constant connection to work"; no full offline mode |
| 2 | **Setup takes effort and is rigid** | 4 | "requires proper setup and training"; "configuration is more rigid than expected"; "a little bit cumbersome" |
| 3 | **Pricing by appointment is hard to predict** | 3 | "very pricey"; "the service appointment limitation can be a challange as some months we have more appointments" |
| 4 | Reports are complex or rigid *(thin)* | 2 | reporting "a little bit complex and cumbersome" |
| 5 | Language: on-screen text in English, or too technical *(thin)* | 2 | some interface text "still in English or too technical"; one reviewer flagged no Arabic support for technicians |

**Problems with the free substitute (WhatsApp and paper).** These come from two vendors who sell WhatsApp tools, so treat them as marketing, not neutral evidence:
- Finished jobs leave no written record, so in a dispute there is "nothing to point back to" ([ChakraHQ](https://chakrahq.com/article/whatsapp-for-home-services-businesses)).
- Group-chat dispatch leads to double bookings and wrong addresses (same source).
- An AMC on a client's ACs can "lapse silently because nobody sent the renewal"; "the first operator to reply with a slot usually wins the visit" ([RichAutomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/best-whatsapp-business-api-for-ac-appliance-repair-india-2026)).
- ChakraHQ's own conclusion is that the gap "is structure, not adoption": shops already live in WhatsApp.

No public reviews were found for TeamShastra, Quick Assign, MyService CRM or Fieldproxy (3 on Capterra India). That is a gap in the research, not proof that their customers are happy.

## 5. The gap

There is no wide-open gap. Cheap, India-built tools for AMC and complaint tracking already exist from ₹0 to ₹99 per technician. What the evidence does point to is one narrow opening:

**Getting the service record out of the technician's head, in the language they speak, without making them learn an app, and sending it to the customer on WhatsApp.**

- The most common complaints about the leading product are a cluttered technician app, the need to be online, rigid setup and English-only text (themes 1, 2 and 5).
- The free substitute fails on records: no written proof of the job and AMC renewals forgotten. Shops won't leave WhatsApp ("structure, not adoption").
- TeamShastra sends PDF reports on WhatsApp, but someone still has to fill in the report. Fieldproxy's AI writes up jobs from **phone calls**, not from what the technician says on site, and does not mention WhatsApp. **No competitor checked advertises Tamil or Tanglish voice-to-report from the field.**
- The prototype already has voice-to-report (100% on action and time, 91.7% on issue, in English, on 12 reports the founder wrote).

How fragile this gap is: it depends on technicians actually wanting to dictate (unproven), on speech-to-text working in Tamil and Tanglish on a noisy job site (not tested; the prototype uses browser speech-to-text in English), and on owners paying more than ₹99 per technician for it. **Failure prediction is not a gap worth chasing**: no competitor in this segment offers it, most likely because household ACs and appliances produce no sensor data.

## 6. The threat: who could copy it fastest

1. **Fieldproxy.** It already has AI agents that write up jobs, a knowledge agent and HVAC customers, and it is backed by Y Combinator. Adding voice notes from technicians and Indian languages is a small step for them. Most dangerous.
2. **TeamShastra.** It already sells to the same kind of buyer at ₹99 per user with WhatsApp PDF reports. Adding a "dictate the report" button would undercut a new entrant on price straight away.
3. **WhatsApp API vendors** (RichAutomate, Wati, AiSensy and others). They already have AI agents inside WhatsApp and pitch AC repair directly. A voice-note-to-job-record bot is close to what they already build.
4. **Zoho.** Slower, but it has Indian distribution, WhatsApp in Premium and its own AI. If voice-to-report becomes something buyers expect, Zoho can add it to the bundle.

**Bottom line:** this is a crowded category with a free floor and cheap Indian incumbents. The business only works if it is the best tool in Chennai at one narrow job (field reports from what technicians say, delivered over WhatsApp), proven on real shops before Fieldproxy or TeamShastra add the same feature.
