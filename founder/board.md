# Board verdict: AITechnician

**Vote: 2 FUND IF, 1 PASS. Average score 3.7 / 10.**

| Lens | Score | Vote |
|---|---|---|
| Offers | 4 | FUND IF |
| Monopoly | 3 | PASS |
| Product | 4 | FUND IF |

Memos: [offers](board/offers.md), [monopoly](board/monopoly.md), [product](board/product.md). Brief: [board/brief.md](board/brief.md).

## Risks raised by more than one member

1. **The AI is proven on the wrong data (all three).** Urgency was trained on IT-helpdesk tickets. Failure prediction was trained on synthetic milling-machine data. The fix-matching and extraction tests are small and written by the founder. Nobody has seen it work on one real Chennai AC, appliance or generator job.
2. **Failure prediction may have no input (all three).** It is not known whether household ACs, appliances or generators produce sensor readings. If they don't, the feature that sounds most defensible does not apply to this market.
3. **The real competitor is WhatsApp, phone and paper, which cost nothing (Offers, Monopoly, Product).** Moving shops onto any software is a behaviour change. What they use and pay today is unknown.
4. **No confirmed customer, no channel, no price (all three).** The "first customer through work or family" is unconfirmed. How customer number 20 arrives is unknown, and the founder builds part-time.
5. **Too broad, outcome vague (Offers, Product).** The pitch is three portals, four models and analytics, not one result an owner can count, such as fewer repeat visits or more jobs per technician per day.

## Where the board disagreed

- **Monopoly passed; Offers and Product did not.** Monopoly sees no technology 10x better than the alternative and no distribution, so it would not fund on conditions. It lists what would reopen the case instead. The other two think a single real pilot is enough to justify continuing.
- **Where to start.** Offers wants to lead with one measurable outcome and a guarantee. Product wants to cut to one feature (voice-to-service-record, or intake and dispatch) and fix the experience. Monopoly cares less about the wedge than about whether real service data can become a moat no copycat has.
- **The customer portal.** Product doubts customers will sign up and register equipment rather than phone or WhatsApp the shop. The other two did not raise this.

## Conditions checklist

Validate first (these come before anything else):
- [ ] **A named first pilot company** agrees to run real jobs through it for 30 to 60 days. (All three)
- [ ] **What target shops use and pay for today** (paper, WhatsApp, Excel, existing FSM software), and who already sells to them. → `/founder-competitors`
- [ ] **Do the target equipment types produce any sensor data?** If not, drop failure prediction from the offer. (All three)
- [ ] **Pick one vertical** (AC, appliance, generator or electrical) and **one wedge feature**; hide the rest from the first customer. (Offers, Product)

During the pilot:
- [ ] **Re-measure** urgency, fix-matching and extraction on the pilot's real complaints and reports, labelled by someone other than the founder, and compare with the current numbers. (All three)
- [ ] **Record where the experience breaks** at each handoff, including voice capture in the field in the language technicians actually speak. (Product)
- [ ] **Beat the shop's current way of working on one metric** the owner cares about (repeat visits, time to close, jobs per technician per day). (Offers, Monopoly)

Before scaling:
- [ ] **Write the one-sentence pitch** and test it on buyers. → `/founder-consumer`
- [ ] **A price owners will pay** that clears hosting and support, not just "cheaper than WhatsApp". → `/founder-pricing`
- [ ] **A guarantee the business can afford**, tied to the outcome. → `/founder-offer`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **Cost to win and support one customer** part-time, and a channel that can be repeated without family connections. → `/founder-cfo`, `/founder-marketing`
- [ ] **3 to 5 shops on live jobs for 60+ days, at least one paying**, before Monopoly reopens. (Monopoly)
- [ ] **A gap the incumbents cannot easily close.** → `/founder-competitors`

## The strongest version the board can see

This is not quite the business pitched. Instead of a full AI field service platform with predictive maintenance, it is **a narrow tool for one kind of Chennai repair shop (say, AC service companies) that turns what technicians say into a finished service record and a customer update, so the owner stops chasing technicians for reports and customers stop calling to ask "what happened?"** It plugs into how jobs already arrive (phone and WhatsApp) instead of asking customers to use a portal. It is proven first on one real company's jobs, then priced against the admin time it saves. Failure prediction and the customer portal wait until there is real data and real demand. If it works, every record collected on local equipment is the start of the data advantage the Monopoly lens is looking for. Without that data, this is one more FSM app in a crowded category.
