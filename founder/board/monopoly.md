# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **The first small market is well defined.** Chennai, 5 to 50 technicians, AC/appliance/generator/electrical repair. That is the right shape for this lens: narrow enough that a big share is possible.
- **A working, end-to-end prototype exists.** Three portals, voice reports and a knowledge base, built solo. The engineering risk of building it is mostly gone. What is left is the business risk.
- **There is a possible path to a data moat.** Every service record, voice report and fix outcome on local equipment is data that generic tools do not have. If enough Chennai shops used it, the urgency, fix and failure models could get better in ways a copycat could not easily match. Right now that is a direction, not an asset.
- **The founder measured the models honestly.** Clear limits and person-in-the-loop design are a good sign for the people question.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No distribution.** The sales channel is undecided, the price is undecided, and the "first customer through work or family" is not confirmed. This lens treats distribution as half the business. Today that half is empty, and the founder builds part-time alongside a day job. How the first 100 customers arrive, and what each costs, is unknown (/founder-cfo, /founder-marketing).
2. **No technology that is 10x better.** Field service management is an existing software category. The AI is not yet better than the next option on anything that matters to these buyers. The urgency model is trained on IT-helpdesk tickets (62% accuracy). The failure model is trained on synthetic milling-machine data. The fix and extraction results come from 40 and 12 examples the founder wrote. On real AC or generator jobs, the product today is a standard FSM app with features that are not yet proven. Who already sells to these shops, and at what price, is open (/founder-competitors).
3. **The real competitor may be WhatsApp and paper.** These cost the buyer nothing, and moving shops off them is a behaviour change, not a feature fight. Also, the failure-prediction feature may have no input at all: it is not known whether household ACs and appliances produce sensor readings. If not, the most defensible-sounding feature does not apply to this market (/founder-consumer).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about Chennai service shops that the existing FSM vendors do not? (The secret.)
- Name the first five companies that would use this. Have any of them seen it?
- How does customer number 20 find you, with no family or work connection?
- What do these shops use today, and what does that cost them per month?
- Where does real service data come from to retrain the models, and how fast?
- Which of the four AI features would a shop owner pay for on its own?
- What stops a funded FSM vendor, or a WhatsApp-based tool, from adding the same features in month six?
- Why now: what has changed that makes these shops ready to switch?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
This is a PASS, not a FUND IF. These are the things that would reopen it:
- 3 to 5 Chennai shops using it on live jobs for 60+ days, with at least one paying.
- Retrained models on that real data that clearly beat the shop's current way of working on one metric the owner cares about (for example, repeat visits or time to close a job).
- A distribution channel that can be repeated, with a cost per customer from /founder-cfo.
- A competitor map from /founder-competitors that shows a gap the incumbents cannot easily close.
