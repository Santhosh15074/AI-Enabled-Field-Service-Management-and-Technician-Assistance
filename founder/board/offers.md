# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A real, everyday pain point.** Small Chennai repair firms with 5 to 50 technicians have to sort incoming complaints, send people out and get reports back from the field. Missed urgent jobs and repeat visits cost them money they can see.
- **The buyers are easy to find.** They are local, they are listed and they are reachable by phone and in person in one city. The Offers lens rates this highly.
- **Some features take work off the buyer.** Voice-dictated reports mean less typing for technicians. Suggested quick fixes can save a visit. Both lower effort instead of adding to it.
- **A working prototype exists.** A buyer could see a live demo, not slides.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nobody has shown the market has both the pain and the money.** Nobody knows how many target firms exist, what they use today (paper, WhatsApp, Excel) or whether they pay for software at all. If the real alternative is free WhatsApp, the offer competes with zero and slides into a price fight. A weak market is the most common reason this lens passes.
2. **Buyers have little reason to believe the results.** None of the AI was tested on real AC, appliance or generator data. The urgency model learned from IT-helpdesk tickets. The failure model learned from synthetic milling-machine data, and it is not known whether the target equipment produces sensor readings at all. The fix and report tests are tiny and were written by the founder. The headline feature, "flags equipment likely to fail", has no proof for this buyer today.
3. **It asks a lot of the buyer, and the outcome is vague.** Three portals mean the owner, the dispatchers, the technicians and the end customers all have to change habits. The pitch describes features, not a result the owner wants, such as fewer repeat visits, more jobs per technician per day or faster payment. With no price, no guarantee and part-time delivery alongside a day job, how fast a buyer sees results is unknown, and support may be slow.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Name one Chennai service-company owner who has said they would pay. Is the "first customer through work or family" real?
- What do these firms use today, and what does it cost them? (/founder-competitors, /founder-consumer)
- What single measurable result can you promise an owner within 30 days? Could you guarantee it?
- Which one vertical comes first: ACs, appliances, generators or electrical? Do those machines give you any sensor data?
- What would you charge, and could you double it and still be the obvious choice? (/founder-pricing)
- What does it cost to win and support one customer while you work full-time elsewhere? (/founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **One confirmed pilot customer** (a named firm, not a hypothetical one) agrees to run it on real jobs for 30 to 60 days.
2. **Re-measure on that firm's real complaints and reports.** Drop failure prediction from the offer until real sensor data exists.
3. **Narrow to one vertical**, and lead with one outcome the owner can count (for example, repeat visits or jobs per technician per day). Run /founder-offer to build the stack and a guarantee the CFO can afford.
4. **Evidence from /founder-consumer and /founder-pricing** that owners in that vertical will pay a price that clears hosting and support costs, so the offer is not "cheaper than WhatsApp."
